Shared learning, contextual specialization

Reaching, contact, transport, precise placement: a single task keeps changing its demands on perception and control.

MorphAct maps the current observation, instruction, and robot state to context-dependent low-rank parameter updates. The same shared generators specialize the policy as the scene changes, without operation labels or separate expert policies.

Y^t=πθ+Δθ(xt)(xt)

xt = (ot, ℓ, st) is the current context; Δθ(xt) is its generated update; Ŷt is the predicted action chunk.

From adapted perception to adapted action

Two generators follow the policy’s computation. The first adapts the vision–language representation. The second reads those adapted features and robot state to generate the action module’s update.

Base weights stay frozen. Generated factors are refreshed at each replanning observation and reused for the entire action chunk.

Generate through adapted representations

Δθv,t=Gv(et;φv)(ht,Ct)=Fv(et;θv+Δθv,t)
ua,t=Ca(ht,st;ρa)Δθa,t=Ga(ua,t;φa)

The adapted VLM produces both the features ht used by the action generator and the native action condition Ct. The generators share their learned rule across contexts, while their outputs change with the observation.

ΔWj,t=αjrjAj,tBj,t

For block j, A: din × r and B: r × dout. The base weights stay frozen; the low-rank factors change with the context.

One action loss, two differentiable routes

The native action loss trains the upstream generator through both the adapted action condition and the generated action parameters.

∇φvℓt=Jrt,φv⊤(gcond+gparam)
gcond=∂ℓt∂rt

Action conditioning

gparam=JΔθa,t,rt⊤∂ℓt∂Δθa,t

Action-side parameter generation

Here rt = (ht, Ct), ℓt is the per-sample native loss, and Jy,z = ∂y/∂z. Both routes remain differentiable. Generated updates are reused throughout the action chunk and refreshed when replanning.

Training objective and factored execution +

Apply the factors directly

y=uWbase+αjrj(uAj,t)Bj,t

The row-vector projection uses a factored residual, without materializing a dense update. Factor layouts follow each backbone’s projection geometry.

Retain the native action objective

minΦ𝔼(xt,Yt)∼𝒟,ξ[ℒnative(y^Φ(xt,Yt;ξ),Yt;ξ)]

Φ includes both generators, the state projection, and projectors retained as trainable by the adaptation configuration. ξ is the randomness used by the native objective.

For π0 and π0.5, training samples a time τ and predicts the velocity on the action–noise path:

zτ=τε+(1−τ)Ytvtarget=ε−Yt

The action generator uses final adapted multimodal tokens and robot state. Layer-wise prefix key–value states provide native action conditioning. Noisy actions and solver time remain in the native action path.

Understanding the need for contextual updates

Shared adaptation can produce competing descent directions across manipulation contexts. The paper examines this interaction through gradient alignment and one-step transfer, measured at a fixed checkpoint.

Gradient alignment

gi(m)=∇θmℒ(Ui;θ)cij(m)=⟨gi(m),gj(m)⟩‖gi(m)‖2‖gj(m)‖2

For module m, negative cosine indicates competing local descent directions across task batches.

One-step transfer

θi+=θ−η∇θℒ(Ui;θ)Zi→j=1−ℒ(Vj;θi+)ℒ(Vj;θ)

A positive transfer score reduces the target loss; a negative score increases it. Every source update starts from the same checkpoint.

All 260 source-improving updates increase loss on other sampled tasks. Cross-task gradient conflict and cancellation are higher in both the VLM and action module. These measurements examine local losses at one fixed checkpoint.

Where conflict concentrates

The action groups outside Transformer blocks, block 00, and block 11 account for 78.88% of pooled negative contribution before rounding. Together, these groups contribute most of the measured negative mass on the action side.
